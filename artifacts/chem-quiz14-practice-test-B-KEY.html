Part B: Coefficients vs. Subscripts (6.1.2, 6.1.3)
The student should have changed the coefficient (the number in front of the whole formula), not the subscript (the small number inside a formula). Changing a subscript changes the identity of the compound — for example, H2O is water but H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide. To balance a reaction you can only adjust how many molecules of each compound you have (coefficients), not what the compounds are (subscripts).

Part B: Define the Mole (6.2.2)
One mole = 6.022 × 1023 particles (Avogadro's Number). Chemists use it because atoms and molecules are far too small to count individually — 6.022 × 1023 is the number of atoms in exactly 12 grams of carbon-12, which links atomic mass units to measurable grams in the lab.

No. Molar Volume (22.4 L/mol) converts between liters of gas and moles — it involves volume, not mass. To convert grams to moles, you must use Molar Mass (44.01 g/mol for CO2). If you wanted to find the volume of CO2 produced at STP, you would use Molar Volume after first finding the moles.
6.3 Multi-Step Stoichiometry
6.3.1 and 6.3.2: Full Calculation
Given: 2 C4H10 + 13 O2 → 8 CO2 + 10 H2O

Road map: L C4H10 → mol C4H10 → mol CO2 → g CO2

Step 1: 2.24 L C4H10 × (1 mol / 22.4 L) = 0.100 mol C4H10
Step 2: 0.100 mol C4H10 × (8 mol CO2 / 2 mol C4H10) = 0.400 mol CO2
Step 3: 0.400 mol CO2 × (44.01 g / 1 mol) = 17.60 g CO2
6.3.3: Conversion Factors
1. Molar Volume (22.4 L/mol at STP) — converts liters of a gas to moles. Used to go from 2.24 L C4H10 → mol C4H10.
2. Mole Ratio (8 mol CO2 / 2 mol C4H10) — from the balanced equation, converts mol of one substance to mol of another.
3. Molar Mass (44.01 g/mol CO2) — converts mol CO2 to grams.
Note: Molar Volume is NOT Molality. Molality (m) = mol solute / kg solvent — a solution concentration unit, completely unrelated.
