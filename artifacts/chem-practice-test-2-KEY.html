Section 4: Multi-Step Stoichiometry (40 pts)
Problem 1 — Haber Process (20 pts)
N2(g) + 3 H2(g) → 2 NH3(g)
Given: 56.0 g N2 and 18.0 g H2.  |  Molar Masses: N2 = 28.02 · H2 = 2.016 · NH3 = 17.03 g/mol
Part (a) — Full Calculation [12 pts · 6.3.1, 6.3.2]
Path 1: Starting from N2 6 pts
56.0 g N2 × (1 mol N2 / 28.02 g) × (2 mol NH3 / 1 mol N2) × (17.03 g NH3 / 1 mol NH3)
= 68.07 g NH3 (accept 67.9 – 68.2 g)
[3 pts correct setup (all 3 factors present), 3 pts correct numeric answer (accept ±0.2)]
Path 2: Starting from H2 6 pts
18.0 g H2 × (1 mol H2 / 2.016 g) × (2 mol NH3 / 3 mol H2) × (17.03 g NH3 / 1 mol NH3)
= 101.37 g NH3 (accept 101.0 – 101.6 g)
[3 pts correct setup, 3 pts correct numeric answer]
Part (b) — Name Your Conversion Factors [3 pts · 6.3.3]
1. Molar Mass of N2 (28.02 g/mol) converts grams of N2 to moles of N2 1 pt
2. Mole Ratio (2 mol NH3 / 1 mol N2) converts moles of N2 to moles of NH3 1 pt
3. Molar Mass of NH3 (17.03 g/mol) converts moles of NH3 to grams of NH3 1 pt
"mol A to mol B" also acceptable for Factor 2 per teacher convention. Name must be correct, not just the number.
Part (c) — Limiting Reagent [5 pts · 6.3.4]
(i) Limiting = N2 (produces 68.07 g NH3 < 101.37 g from H2)  |  Excess = H2 2 pts
[1 pt each]. Identifying LR as H2 = 0 pts (H2 gives MORE NH3, so it is excess).
(ii) 3 pts
H2 consumed:
56.0 g N2 × (1 mol N2 / 28.02 g) × (3 mol H2 / 1 mol N2) × (2.016 g H2 / 1 mol H2)
= 12.09 g H2 consumed
H2 remaining = 18.0 − 12.09 = 5.91 g H2 (accept 5.8 – 6.0 g)
[2 pts setup, 1 pt correct answer]. Student may also back-calculate from grams of NH3 produced (68.07 g) using NH3:H2 ratio — also valid.
Problem 2 — Aluminum Chloride Synthesis (20 pts)
2 Al(s) + 3 Cl2(g) → 2 AlCl3(s)
Given: 27.0 g Al and 85.0 g Cl2.  |  Molar Masses: Al = 26.98 · Cl2 = 70.90 · AlCl3 = 133.33 g/mol
Part (a) — Full Calculation [12 pts · 6.3.1, 6.3.2]
Path 1: Starting from Al 6 pts
27.0 g Al × (1 mol Al / 26.98 g) × (2 mol AlCl3 / 2 mol Al) × (133.33 g AlCl3 / 1 mol AlCl3)
= 133.4 g AlCl3 (accept 133.0 – 133.6 g)
Note: 2 mol AlCl3 / 2 mol Al simplifies to 1:1 ratio.
[3 pts correct setup, 3 pts correct numeric answer]
Path 2: Starting from Cl2 6 pts
85.0 g Cl2 × (1 mol Cl2 / 70.90 g) × (2 mol AlCl3 / 3 mol Cl2) × (133.33 g AlCl3 / 1 mol AlCl3)
= 106.6 g AlCl3 (accept 106.2 – 107.0 g)
[3 pts correct setup, 3 pts correct numeric answer]
Part (b) — Name Your Conversion Factors [3 pts · 6.3.3]
1. Molar Mass of Al (26.98 g/mol) converts grams of Al to moles of Al 1 pt
2. Mole Ratio (2 mol AlCl3 / 2 mol Al) converts moles of Al to moles of AlCl3 1 pt
3. Molar Mass of AlCl3 (133.33 g/mol) converts moles of AlCl3 to grams of AlCl3 1 pt
Part (c) — Limiting Reagent [5 pts · 6.3.4]
(i) Limiting = Cl2 (produces 106.6 g AlCl3 < 133.4 g from Al)  |  Excess = Al 2 pts
Common error trap: Cl2 is listed second — student must check the math, not assume first-listed = LR. [1 pt each]
(ii) 3 pts
Al consumed:
85.0 g Cl2 × (1 mol Cl2 / 70.90 g) × (2 mol Al / 3 mol Cl2) × (26.98 g Al / 1 mol Al)
= 21.56 g Al consumed
Al remaining = 27.0 − 21.56 = 5.44 g Al (accept 5.3 – 5.6 g)
[2 pts setup, 1 pt correct answer]. Student may also use Al produced from Path 1 and back-calculate via Al:Cl2 ratio — valid per same logic as Kai's corrected method on the comprehensive re-test.

Section 5: Molarity and Concentration 7.1.4 (10 pts)
Problem 1 (5 pts)
42.5 g KNO3 in 500. mL solution. Molar mass KNO3 = 101.10 g/mol.
42.5 g ÷ 101.10 g/mol = 0.4204 mol KNO3
M = 0.4204 mol ÷ 0.500 L = 0.841 M (accept 0.840 – 0.842 M)
[2 pts mol calc, 2 pts M = mol/L, 1 pt correct answer]. Forgetting mL → L = deduct 1 pt.
Problem 2 (5 pts)
Prepare 250. mL of 0.600 M CaCl2. Molar mass CaCl2 = 110.98 g/mol.
mol CaCl2 = 0.600 mol/L × 0.250 L = 0.150 mol
g CaCl2 = 0.150 mol × 110.98 g/mol = 16.65 g CaCl2 (accept 16.6 – 16.7 g)
[2 pts mol = M × V, 2 pts g = mol × MM, 1 pt correct answer]. Forgetting mL → L = deduct 1 pt.
