Part B: Coefficients vs Subscripts (6.1.2, 6.1.3) — Kai: NP
You can CHANGE the coefficient (the number in front of the formula) because it only changes how many molecules of that substance are present without changing the substance itself. You CANNOT change the subscript because changing a subscript changes the identity of the compound (e.g., CO vs CO2 are different substances). The coefficient adjusts quantity; the subscript defines identity.

6.3.4: Limiting Reagent — 10.0 g H2 + 10.0 g N2 → NH3 — Kai: NP (BLANK)
Reaction: 3 H2(g) + N2(g) → 2 NH3(g)
Step 1: Convert to moles
H2: 10.0 g / 2.016 g/mol = 4.96 mol H2
N2: 10.0 g / 28.02 g/mol = 0.357 mol N2

Step 2: Find limiting reagent
From stoichiometry: 1 mol N2 needs 3 mol H2
0.357 mol N2 needs 0.357 × 3 = 1.071 mol H2 (we have 4.96, plenty)
4.96 mol H2 needs 4.96 / 3 = 1.653 mol N2 (we only have 0.357!)

N2 is the Limiting Reagent
H2 is the Excess Reagent

Step 3: Maximum yield of NH3
0.357 mol N2 × (2 mol NH3 / 1 mol N2) = 0.714 mol NH3
0.714 × 17.03 g/mol = 12.15 g NH3

Step 4: Excess H2 remaining
H2 used: 0.357 × 3 = 1.071 mol × 2.016 g/mol = 2.16 g
Remaining: 10.0 - 2.16 = 7.84 g H2 remaining
