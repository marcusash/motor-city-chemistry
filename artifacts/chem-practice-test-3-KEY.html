Section 4: Stoichiometry — Limiting and Excess Reagents (40 pts)
Problem 1 — Iron Oxide Formation (20 pts)  6.3.16.3.26.3.4
4 Fe(s)  +  3 O2(g)  →  2 Fe2O3(s)
Molar Masses (from periodic table):  Fe = 55.85  ·  O2 = 32.00  ·  Fe2O3 = 159.70 g/mol

IF 50.0 g of Fe and 40.0 g of O2, were to react, calculate the maximum yield of Fe2O3 that could be produced:

Path A — via Fe:
50.0 g Fe × (1 mol Fe / 55.85 g) × (2 mol Fe₂O₃ / 4 mol Fe) × (159.70 g / 1 mol Fe₂O₃) = 71.49 g Fe₂O₃
Path B — via O₂:
40.0 g O₂ × (1 mol O₂ / 32.00 g) × (2 mol Fe₂O₃ / 3 mol O₂) × (159.70 g / 1 mol Fe₂O₃) = 133.1 g Fe₂O₃
Path A < Path B → Fe is the Limiting Reagent → maximum yield = 71.49 g Fe₂O₃

Rubric: 4 pts each path (DA setup + mole ratio + arithmetic) · 2 pts LR identification · max yield = Path A answer

Which substance is the Limiting Reagent?  (circle one)   FeO2Fe2O3

Which substance is the Excess Reagent?  (circle one)   FeO2Fe2O3

Calculate the remaining amount (in grams) of the excess Reagent:

O₂ consumed: 50.0 g Fe × (1 mol Fe / 55.85 g) × (3 mol O₂ / 4 mol Fe) × (32.00 g / 1 mol O₂) = 21.49 g O₂ used
Remaining O₂: 40.0 − 21.49 = 18.51 g O₂

Rubric: 4 pts DA setup · 2 pts subtraction · accept 18.5 g

Problem 2 — Sodium Chloride Synthesis (20 pts)  6.3.16.3.26.3.4
2 Na(s)  +  Cl2(g)  →  2 NaCl(s)
Molar Masses (from periodic table):  Na = 22.99  ·  Cl2 = 70.90  ·  NaCl = 58.44 g/mol

IF 35.0 g of Na and 85.0 g of Cl2, were to react, calculate the maximum yield of NaCl that could be produced:

Path A — via Na:
35.0 g Na × (1 mol Na / 22.99 g) × (2 mol NaCl / 2 mol Na) × (58.44 g / 1 mol NaCl) = 88.97 g NaCl
Path B — via Cl₂:
85.0 g Cl₂ × (1 mol Cl₂ / 70.90 g) × (2 mol NaCl / 1 mol Cl₂) × (58.44 g / 1 mol NaCl) = 140.1 g NaCl
Path A < Path B → Na is the Limiting Reagent → maximum yield = 88.97 g NaCl

Rubric: 4 pts each path (DA setup + mole ratio + arithmetic) · 2 pts LR identification · max yield = Path A answer

Which substance is the Limiting Reagent?  (circle one)   NaCl2NaCl

Which substance is the Excess Reagent?  (circle one)   NaCl2NaCl

Calculate the remaining amount (in grams) of the excess Reagent:

Cl₂ consumed: 35.0 g Na × (1 mol Na / 22.99 g) × (1 mol Cl₂ / 2 mol Na) × (70.90 g / 1 mol Cl₂) = 53.97 g Cl₂ used
Remaining Cl₂: 85.0 − 53.97 = 31.03 g Cl₂

Rubric: 4 pts DA setup · 2 pts subtraction · accept 31.0 g

Section 5: Molarity and Concentration  7.1.4 (10 pts)

Problem 1 (5 pts)

29.2 g of NaCl is dissolved in enough water to make 400. mL of solution. What is the molarity of this solution?

MM NaCl = 22.99 + 35.45 = 58.44 g/mol
mol NaCl = 29.2 g ÷ 58.44 g/mol = 0.4996 mol
M = mol ÷ L = 0.4996 mol ÷ 0.400 L = 1.25 M

Rubric: 1 pt MM · 2 pts mol calc · 2 pts M calc · accept 1.249 M or 1.25 M

Problem 2 (5 pts)

You need to prepare 350. mL of a 0.250 M BaCl2 solution. How many grams of BaCl2 do you need?

MM BaCl₂ = 137.33 + 2(35.45) = 208.23 g/mol
mol BaCl₂ = 0.350 L × 0.250 mol/L = 0.08750 mol
mass = 0.08750 mol × 208.23 g/mol = 18.22 g BaCl₂

Rubric: 1 pt MM · 2 pts mol calc · 2 pts mass calc · accept 18.2 g
