Section 1: Molecular Shape

1. The central atom in water (H₂O) has 2 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs. What is the total number of negative charge centers on the central oxygen?
A) 2 B) 3 C) 4 D) 6
2. Based on 4 charge centers with 2 lone pairs, what is the molecular shape of H₂O?
A) Tetrahedral B) Trigonal pyramidal C) Bent D) Linear
3. What is the approximate bond angle in a bent molecule like H₂O?
A) 90° B) ~104.5° C) 120° D) 180°
4. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) has 2 double bonds and 0 lone pairs on carbon. Its molecular shape is:
A) Bent B) Trigonal planar C) Linear D) Tetrahedral
5. What is the bond angle in CO₂?
A) 90° B) 109.5° C) 120° D) 180°
6. NH₃ has 3 bonding pairs and 1 lone pair on nitrogen. Its molecular shape is:
A) Trigonal planar B) Trigonal pyramidal C) Tetrahedral D) Bent
7. The bond angle in NH₃ is ~107°, slightly less than the ideal 109.5°. Why?
A) The N–H bonds are longer than normal B) The lone pair occupies more space than a bonding pair, compressing the bond angles C) The nitrogen atom is too small to hold 4 pairs D) The bonds are ionic
8. BeCl₂ has 2 bonding pairs and 0 lone pairs on beryllium. Its shape is:
A) Bent B) Trigonal planar C) Tetrahedral D) Linear
9. A central atom with 4 bonding pairs and 0 lone pairs has which geometry?
A) Bent B) Trigonal pyramidal C) Tetrahedral D) Trigonal planar
10. What bond angle is associated with trigonal planar geometry?
A) 90° B) 109.5° C) 120° D) 180°
Section 2: Polarity & Intermolecular Forces

Choose the best answer for each question. Mark only one answer per item.

11. The electronegativity of N = 3.0 and H = 2.1. The N–H bond is:
A) Nonpolar covalent (ΔEN = 0) B) Polar covalent (ΔEN = 0.9) C) Ionic (ΔEN > 1.7) D) Metallic
12. The C–C bond has ΔEN = 0. It is:
A) Ionic B) Polar covalent C) Nonpolar covalent D) A hydrogen bond
13. In the C–O bond, oxygen is more electronegative (EN = 3.5 vs. C = 2.5). The polarity arrow points:
A) From O to C (toward the less electronegative atom) B) From C to O (toward the more electronegative atom) C) There is no polarity arrow — the bond is nonpolar D) In both directions simultaneously
14. CO₂ has 2 polar C=O bonds arranged in a straight line (180°). The molecule is:
A) Polar — it has polar bonds B) Nonpolar — the symmetric arrangement cancels the dipoles C) Ionic — large electronegativity difference D) Unable to interact with other molecules
15. H₂O is polar even though both O–H bonds are identical. This is because:
A) The bonds are ionic B) The bent shape + lone pairs create an asymmetric charge distribution — net dipole remains C) Oxygen is too large to be symmetric D) The bond angles are exactly 180°
16. Which intermolecular force is present in ALL molecular substances regardless of polarity?
A) Hydrogen bonding B) Dipole-dipole forces C) London dispersion forces D) Covalent bonds
17. For hydrogen bonding to occur, hydrogen must be covalently bonded directly to:
A) Carbon or Sulfur B) N, O, or F C) Any atom that has lone pairs D) A metal atom
18. Which molecule experiences the strongest intermolecular forces?
A) CH₄ B) HF C) Cl₂ D) N₂
19. Arrange by INCREASING boiling point: CH₄ (LDF only), HCl (dipole-dipole), H₂O (H-bonding)
A) H₂O < HCl < CH₄ B) HCl < CH₄ < H₂O C) CH₄ < HCl < H₂O D) CH₄ < H₂O < HCl
20. HF has a much higher boiling point than HCl, even though HCl has a greater molar mass. The best explanation is:
A) HF has stronger London dispersion forces B) HF molecules can form hydrogen bonds with each other; HCl cannot C) HCl is an ionic compound D) HF molecules are smaller and move more slowly
21. "Like dissolves like" means:
A) Substances with similar molar masses always mix B) Polar solutes dissolve in polar solvents; nonpolar solutes dissolve in nonpolar solvents C) Ionic compounds only dissolve in ionic solvents D) All molecular compounds dissolve in water
22. Which substance would dissolve most readily in a nonpolar solvent like hexane?
A) NaCl (ionic) B) HF (polar, H-bonding) C) H₂O (polar, H-bonding) D) I₂ (nonpolar)
