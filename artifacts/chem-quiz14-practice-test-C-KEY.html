Key: multiply coefficient × each element inside parentheses × subscript outside parentheses.
Ca(NO3)2: the subscript 2 outside applies to the whole NO3 group → 2 N, 6 O per formula unit. ×2 → 4 N, 12 O.
(NH4)2CO3: the subscript 2 outside applies to NH4 → 2 N, 8 H. The CO3 adds 3 O → total 3 O.
Part B: Conceptual (6.1.2, 6.1.3)
Accept any valid simple equation. Model answer:
Skeleton (unbalanced): H2 + O2 → H2O
Balanced: 2 H2 + O2 → 2 H2O

I changed the coefficients (the numbers in front of reactants and products) to balance the equation. I could not change a subscript — for example, writing H2O2 instead of H2O — because that would change the identity of the substance itself. Subscripts define what molecule we are talking about. Coefficients only change how many molecules of each substance are present, not what they are.

6.2 Converting Chemical Units
Part A: kg → g (6.2.1)
2750 g
2.75 kg × (1000 g / 1 kg) = 2750 g
Part B: Avogadro's Number (6.2.2)
6.022 × 1023 is Avogadro's number — the number of particles (atoms, molecules, ions) in one mole of a substance. Chemists use it because atoms are too small to count individually. Even counting 1 billion atoms per second, it would take over 19 million years to count one mole. Avogadro's number gives us a bridge between the atomic scale and amounts we can measure in a lab.
Part C: Molar Mass (6.2.4)

C3H8 44.09 CO2 44.01

CO2: 12.01 + 2(16.00) = 44.01 g/mol

Part D: Conversions

1. 0.375 mol H2O → molecules (6.2.3)   Conversion factor: Avogadro's Number

2.26 × 1023 molecules
0.375 mol × (6.022 × 1023 molecules / 1 mol) = 2.258 × 1023 molecules

2. 22.0 g CO2 → moles (6.2.5)   Conversion factor: Molar Mass

0.500 mol
22.0 g × (1 mol / 44.01 g) = 0.4999 ≈ 0.500 mol
Note: divide by molar mass to go g → mol (opposite of mol → g).

3. 0.60 mol C3H8 → mol O2 (6.2.6)   Conversion factor: Mole Ratio

3.0 mol O2
0.60 mol C3H8 × (5 mol O2 / 1 mol C3H8) = 3.0 mol O2
Ratio from balanced equation: 1 mol C3H8 requires 5 mol O2.

4. Molar Volume for liquid H2O (6.2.7)

No. Molar Volume (22.4 L/mol) only applies to gases at STP — it describes the volume one mole of an ideal gas occupies at standard temperature and pressure. Liquid water does not behave like a gas. To find the volume of 0.10 mol of liquid H2O, convert to grams using molar mass (18.02 g/mol → 1.80 g), then use the density of water (1.00 g/mL) to get 1.80 mL.

6.3 Multi-Step Stoichiometry

C3H8(g) + 5 O2(g) → 3 CO2(g) + 4 H2O(g)

5.80 g C3H8 → ? g H2O

(6.3.1) Full calculation:
9.48 g H2O
Step 1 — g C3H8 → mol C3H8:
5.80 g C3H8 × (1 mol C3H8 / 44.09 g C3H8) = 0.1315 mol C3H8
Step 2 — mol C3H8 → mol H2O (mole ratio):
0.1315 mol C3H8 × (4 mol H2O / 1 mol C3H8) = 0.5261 mol H2O
Step 3 — mol H2O → g H2O:
0.5261 mol H2O × (18.02 g H2O / 1 mol H2O) = 9.48 g H2O
(6.3.3) Conversion Factors Used:
1. Molar Mass of C3H8 (44.09 g/mol) — converts grams of propane to moles of propane
2. Mole Ratio (4 mol H2O per 1 mol C3H8, from balanced equation) — converts moles of propane to moles of water
3. Molar Mass of H2O (18.02 g/mol) — converts moles of water to grams of water
(6.3.4) Limiting Reagent: N2 + 3 H2 → 2 NH3

Given: 5.6 g N2, 6.0 g H2

Convert both to moles:
mol N2 = 5.6 g ÷ 28.02 g/mol = 0.200 mol
mol H2 = 6.0 g ÷ 2.016 g/mol = 2.976 mol
Identify limiting reagent:
To consume 0.200 mol N2, need 0.200 × 3 = 0.600 mol H2
Available H2: 2.976 mol > 0.600 mol needed → N2 is the Limiting Reagent
Maximum NH3 yield:
0.200 mol N2 × (2 mol NH3 / 1 mol N2) = 0.400 mol NH3
0.400 mol × 17.03 g/mol = 6.81 g NH3
Excess H2 remaining:
H2 used: 0.600 mol → 0.600 × 2.016 = 1.21 g used
H2 remaining: 2.976 − 0.600 = 2.376 mol × 2.016 g/mol = 4.79 g H2
