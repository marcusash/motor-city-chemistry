Q6 — H2O Shape (Table) 5.1.2 shape names conceptual gap −2 pts

Q6 table row: H2O has 2 bonding sites and 2 lone pairs. Name the molecular shape.

Kai's Answer
Linear
Correct Answer
Tetrahedral Bent (or Bent)
Root Cause
Linear is the shape for 2 bonding sites with zero lone pairs. H2O has 2 lone pairs that push the two H atoms into a bent geometry. The lone pairs compress the angle to ~104.5°. This is distinct from "linear" (180°) because the electron domain geometry is tetrahedral, not linear. The shape name "Tetrahedral Bent" or simply "Bent" comes from counting only the atom positions, not the lone pairs. This is the most important conceptual fix before Quiz B.
Q5a — 3 Bonds + 1 Lone Pair Shape 5.1.2 shape names likely careless −2 pts

Q5a: A central atom with 3 bonding sites and 1 lone pair — name the molecular shape.

Kai's Answer
Trigonal Planar
Correct Answer
Trigonal Pyramidal
Root Cause
Likely a careless read: Kai answered "Trigonal Planar" here but correctly wrote "Trigonal Pyramidal" for NH3 in Q6 two questions later — NH3 is exactly this molecule. Trigonal Planar is 3 bonds + 0 lone pairs (BF3, SO3). The lone pair in Trigonal Pyramidal pushes the three bonding pairs down, making the molecule look like a triangular base with the central atom sitting above it — like a pyramid. This is not flat (planar).

Q3c: How does SO2's electron domain geometry make it different from SO3?

Kai's Answer

Full Answer
SO2 has 2 bonding sites + 1 lone pair → Trigonal Planar Bent (~119°). SO3 has 3 bonding sites + 0 lone pairs → Trigonal Planar (120°). The lone pair in SO2 changes the shape and compresses the angle.
Root Cause
Kai identified the bonding-site difference but didn't connect it to the shape consequence. The rule: same number of electron domains can give different shapes depending on how many are lone pairs vs. bonds.
Q4b — Tetrahedral Bond Angle 5.1.2 bond angles −1 pt

Q4b fill-in: 4 bonding sites, 0 lone pairs → shape and bond angle.

Kai's Answer

Correct Answer
Tetrahedral, 109.5°
Root Cause
107° is the bond angle for Trigonal Pyramidal (4 electron domains, but 1 is a lone pair). The ideal Tetrahedral angle is 109.5°. Memorizing the two different angles and why they differ is key: 109.5° (perfect tetrahedral, no LP compression) vs. ~107° (LP pushes bonds slightly closer). Shape name is correct — shape and angle are one package to memorize together.

PRACTICE PLAN — BEFORE QUIZ B
Priority 1: H2O is BENT, not Linear. — 2 bonds + 2 lone pairs = Tetrahedral Bent (~104.5°). Make this a flashcard. The rule: "if it has lone pairs, the shape is not what the electron domains suggest." Drill this molecule until it's automatic.
Priority 2: Tetrahedral angle = 109.5°, Trig Pyramidal = ~107°. — Memorize as a pair. 109.5° is the "ideal" 4-domain angle with no lone pair pushing. 107° is what you get when 1 lone pair compresses it. Both shapes have 4 electron domains; only the name and angle differ because of the lone pair count.
Priority 3: Shape explanation must state "atom positions only." — When asked to explain why a shape is named a certain way, always say: "Molecular shape is named by where the atoms are, not where the lone pairs are." Then explain the lone pair's mechanical effect. Both pieces must be present.
Priority 4: SO2 vs SO3 — connect bonding sites to shape consequence. — Different lone pair count → different shapes AND different angles. When the question asks "how is X different from Y," always include: bonding sites, lone pairs, shape name, and angle.
