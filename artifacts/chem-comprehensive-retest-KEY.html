Section 2: Bond Polarity and Molecular Polarity (16 pts)
Electronegativity (EN) values: H = 2.1  |  C = 2.5  |  N = 3.0  |  O = 3.5  |  F = 4.0  |  Cl = 3.0  |  S = 2.5  |  P = 2.1
Molecule A: HCl (8 pts)
δ+ H — :Cl: δ−
(a) ΔEN = 3.0 − 2.1 = 0.9 → POLAR BOND 2 pts
[1 pt for ΔEN calc, 1 pt for polar label]
(b) H = δ+ (less EN), Cl = δ− (more EN) 2 pts
[1 pt each atom labeled correctly]. If H labeled as δ− = 0 pts. Must show δ symbol OR arrow.
(c) POLAR. HCl is linear with one polar bond. There is no second bond to cancel it. 4 pts
[2 pts for "polar" claim, 2 pts for justification mentioning single bond or no cancellation]
Molecule B: CO2 (8 pts)
δ− :O: = δ+ C δ+ = :O: δ−
(Each C end is δ+, each O end is δ−)
(a) ΔEN = 3.5 − 2.5 = 1.0 → POLAR BONDS 2 pts
[1 pt for ΔEN calc, 1 pt for polar label]
(b) Both O atoms must be δ−; C must be δ+ 2 pts
[1 pt each side labeled correctly]
(c) NONPOLAR. CO2 is linear and symmetric — the two equal polar bonds point in opposite directions and cancel each other out. 4 pts
[2 pts for "nonpolar" claim, 2 pts for justification mentioning SYMMETRY or CANCELLATION]. Saying "nonpolar because bonds are nonpolar" = 0 pts for (c) (wrong reason — bonds ARE polar).

Part B — Rank Boiling Points (10 pts)
D > B > E > C > A
H2O > HF > HCl > CH4 > N2
Justification: H2O has the strongest IMFs (extensive hydrogen bonding network with 2 donor H's per molecule), so it requires the most energy to break apart and has the highest boiling point.
Scoring (Part B): Correct full ranking = 8 pts (partial credit: 1 pt per correct adjacent pair, max 4 pairs). Justification = 2 pts (must mention HB as strongest force in H2O, or correctly explain why H2O is highest).
Acceptable variant: D > B > E > A > C if student correctly argues CH4 vs N2 differently. At this level, either order for the two nonpolars is acceptable with justification. Full 8 pts if D > B > E is correct and last two are in defensible order.

Section 4: Multi-Step Stoichiometry (24 pts)
Fe2O3(s) + 3 CO(g) → 2 Fe(s) + 3 CO2(g)
Given: 40.0 g Fe2O3 and 28.0 g CO available.  |  Molar Masses: Fe2O3 = 159.69 · CO = 28.01 · Fe = 55.85 · CO2 = 44.01 g/mol
Part (a) — Full Calculation [12 pts · 6.3.1, 6.3.2]
Path 1: Starting from Fe2O3 6 pts
40.0 g Fe2O3 × (1 mol Fe2O3 / 159.69 g) × (2 mol Fe / 1 mol Fe2O3) × (55.85 g Fe / 1 mol Fe)
= 27.97 g Fe (accept 27.8 – 28.0 g)
[3 pts correct conversion factor setup (all 3 factors present), 3 pts correct numeric answer (accept ±0.2)]
Path 2: Starting from CO 6 pts
28.0 g CO × (1 mol CO / 28.01 g) × (2 mol Fe / 3 mol CO) × (55.85 g Fe / 1 mol Fe)
= 37.22 g Fe (accept 37.0 – 37.4 g)
[3 pts correct conversion factor setup, 3 pts correct numeric answer]
Part (b) — Name Your Conversion Factors [6 pts · 6.3.3]
1. Molar Mass (of Fe2O3, 159.69 g/mol) converts grams of Fe2O3 to moles of Fe2O3 2 pts
2. Mole Ratio (2 mol Fe / 1 mol Fe2O3) converts moles of Fe2O3 to moles of Fe 2 pts
3. Molar Mass (of Fe, 55.85 g/mol) converts moles of Fe to grams of Fe 2 pts
"Molality" = NP. "Molar Volume" = NP (no gas conversion needed; no liters involved). "Dimensional analysis" = NP (method, not factor name). Name must be correct, not just the number.
Part (c) — Limiting Reagent [6 pts · 6.3.4]
(i) Limiting = Fe2O3 (produces 27.97 g Fe < 37.22 g from CO)  |  Excess = CO 2 pts
[1 pt each]. Identifying LR as CO = 0 pts (CO gives MORE Fe, so it is excess).
(ii) 4 pts
CO consumed:
0.2504 mol Fe2O3 × (3 mol CO / 1 mol Fe2O3) × (28.01 g CO / 1 mol CO)
= 0.2504 × 3 × 28.01 = 21.05 g CO consumed
CO remaining = 28.0 − 21.05 = 6.95 g CO (accept 6.9 – 7.0 g)
[2 pts setup (correct factors), 2 pts correct answer]. Calculating remaining Fe instead = 0 pts for (ii).

Grading Notes for the Key

5.1.2 Shape name common errors:

  • "Pyramidal tetrahedral" → accept as Trigonal Pyramidal (terminology, award 1/2)
  • "Trigonal Planar" for PCl3 → NP (does not account for LP)
  • "Bent" without "Tetrahedral" for OF2 → award 1/2 pts

5.1.7 Angle errors:

  • 120° for PCl3 → NP (ignores LP compression)
  • 109.5° for OF2 → NP (this is the IDEAL, not the actual; two LP compress further than one)
  • Accept "~107°" or "107°" for PCl3; accept "~104.5°" or "104.5°" for OF2

5.1.6 Polarity arrow errors:

  • Labels δ+ on Cl in HCl → NP for that bond
  • Says "nonpolar" for CO2 because "bonds are nonpolar" → NP for justification (wrong reason)

6.3.3 Conversion factor name errors:

  • "Molality" instead of "Molar Mass" or "Molar Volume" → NP
  • "Dimensional analysis" (describes method, not factor name) → NP

6.3.4 Limiting reagent:

  • Identifies LR as CO → NP (CO gives MORE Fe, so it is excess)
  • Calculates remaining Fe instead of remaining CO → NP for 6.3.4(ii)

7.1.4 Molarity:

  • Inverts M = mol/L formula → NP
  • Forgets unit conversion mL → L → deduct 1 pt but award 4 if answer otherwise correct
