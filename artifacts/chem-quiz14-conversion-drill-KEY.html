6.2 — Stoichiometric Conversions
Problem 1 — Glucose Combustion
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
Step 1 — Molar mass of glucose
6(12.01) + 12(1.008) + 6(16.00) = 72.06 + 12.10 + 96.00 = 180.16 g/mol
Step 2 — g glucose → mol glucose
45.0 g × (1 mol / 180.16 g) = 0.2498 mol C₆H₁₂O₆
Step 3 — mol glucose → mol CO₂ (molar ratio)
0.2498 mol × (6 mol CO₂ / 1 mol C₆H₁₂O₆) = 1.499 mol CO₂
Step 4 — mol CO₂ → g CO₂
MM CO₂ = 12.01 + 2(16.00) = 44.01 g/mol
1.499 mol × 44.01 g/mol = 65.96 g
66.0 g CO₂
Problem 2 — Magnesium Combustion
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
Step 1 — Molar mass of Mg
MM Mg = 24.31 g/mol
Step 2 — g Mg → mol Mg
12.0 g × (1 mol / 24.31 g) = 0.4936 mol Mg
Step 3 — mol Mg → mol MgO (molar ratio)
0.4936 mol × (2 mol MgO / 2 mol Mg) = 0.4936 mol MgO
Step 4 — mol MgO → g MgO
MM MgO = 24.31 + 16.00 = 40.31 g/mol
0.4936 mol × 40.31 g/mol = 19.90 g
19.9 g MgO

Molar ratio is 2:2 (= 1:1), so mol MgO = mol Mg exactly.

6.1 — Balancing
Problem 3 — Acetylene Combustion
C₂H₂ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
Step 1 — Balance C
2 C on left → need 2CO₂ on right
Step 2 — Balance H
2 H on left → need 1H₂O on right
Step 3 — Balance O
Right: 2(2) + 1(1) = 5 O atoms → need 5/2 O₂ on left
C₂H₂ + 5/2 O₂ → 2CO₂ + H₂O
Step 4 — Multiply through by 2 to clear fraction
× 2 each coefficient
2C₂H₂ + 5O₂ → 4CO₂ + 2H₂O

Check: C = 4 = 4  |  H = 4 = 4  |  O = 10 = 10  ✓
