Section A: Ionic or Covalent? Name It.
The Key Rule

Metal + nonmetal = ionic. No prefixes. Just metal name + nonmetal-ide.

Nonmetal + nonmetal = covalent. Use prefixes (mono-, di-, tri-, tetra-, penta-).

How to tell: Metals are on the LEFT side of the periodic table (groups 1A-3A, plus transition metals). Nonmetals are on the RIGHT (groups 4A-8A). If the first element is a metal, it's ionic.

#1: CaF2
Hint

Ca = calcium. Is calcium a metal or nonmetal? (Check the periodic table: group 2A.) F = fluorine (group 7A, nonmetal).

Full Answer
Ca is a metal (group 2A). F is a nonmetal (group 7A).
Metal + nonmetal = ionic. No prefixes.
Metal name: calcium. Nonmetal root + -ide: fluoride.
Ionic | calcium fluoride

NOT "calcium difluoride." The "di-" is wrong because this is ionic.

#2: N2O3
Hint

N = nitrogen. O = oxygen. Are BOTH of these nonmetals? If yes, what naming system do you use?

Full Answer
N is a nonmetal (group 5A). O is a nonmetal (group 6A).
Nonmetal + nonmetal = covalent. Use prefixes!
2 nitrogen = dinitrogen. 3 oxygen = trioxide.
Covalent | dinitrogen trioxide
#3: Li2S
Hint

Li = lithium (group 1A). S = sulfur (group 6A). Metal or nonmetal?

Full Answer
Li is a metal (group 1A). S is a nonmetal (group 6A).
Metal + nonmetal = ionic. No prefixes.
Metal name: lithium. Nonmetal root + -ide: sulfide.
Ionic | lithium sulfide

NOT "dilithium sulfide." No prefixes for ionic.

#4: CO
Hint

C = carbon (group 4A). O = oxygen (group 6A). Both nonmetals? Then which naming system?

Special note: when there is only ONE of the FIRST element, you drop the "mono-" prefix on the first element. But you still use "mono-" on the second element if there's only one.

Full Answer
C is a nonmetal. O is a nonmetal.
Nonmetal + nonmetal = covalent. Use prefixes!
1 carbon = carbon (drop "mono-" on first element). 1 oxygen = monoxide.
Covalent | carbon monoxide
Mono- Rule

Never use "mono-" on the FIRST element. "Monocarbon monoxide" is wrong. But DO use "mono-" on the second element when there's only one of it. "Carbon monoxide" is correct.

#5: SrCl2
Hint

Sr = strontium. Look it up on the periodic table. What group is it in? Is it a metal?

Full Answer
Sr is strontium (group 2A, metal). Cl is chlorine (group 7A, nonmetal).
Metal + nonmetal = ionic. No prefixes.
Metal name: strontium. Nonmetal root + -ide: chloride.
Ionic | strontium chloride
#6: P2O5
Hint

P = phosphorus (group 5A). O = oxygen (group 6A). Both nonmetals?

Full Answer
P is a nonmetal (group 5A). O is a nonmetal (group 6A).
Nonmetal + nonmetal = covalent. Use prefixes!
2 phosphorus = diphosphorus. 5 oxygen = pentoxide.
Covalent | diphosphorus pentoxide
Prefix Spelling

"Pentoxide" not "pentaoxide." When the prefix ends in "a" or "o" and the element starts with "a" or "o", you drop the extra vowel. Penta + oxide = pentoxide. Mono + oxide = monoxide.

Section B: Transition Metal Compounds
How to Find the Metal's Charge

1. Find the total negative charge from the anion(s).

2. The total positive charge must be equal and opposite (to make the compound neutral).

3. Divide by the number of metal atoms to get one metal's charge.

4. That charge becomes the Roman numeral.

#7: CuCl2
Hint

Cu = copper (transition metal). Cl = chlorine (group 7A, charge = -1). There are 2 Cl atoms. What's the total negative charge? What must Cu's charge be?

Full Answer
Cl is -1. There are 2 Cl: total negative = (-1)(2) = -2.
Compound must be neutral. Cu must be +2.
Cu is +2, so Roman numeral = (II). Cl root + -ide = chloride.
Cu = +2 | copper(II) chloride
#8: Fe2O3
Hint

O = oxygen (group 6A, charge = -2). There are 3 O atoms. Total negative charge? There are 2 Fe atoms. If total positive must cancel total negative, what's the total positive? Now divide by 2 to get ONE iron's charge.

Full Answer
O is -2. There are 3 O: total negative = (-2)(3) = -6.
Total positive must be +6. There are 2 Fe: each Fe = +6 / 2 = +3.
Fe is +3, so Roman numeral = (III). O root + -ide = oxide.
Fe = +3 | iron(III) oxide
This is NOT the same as FeCl₂

In FeCl2, iron is +2 (II). In Fe2O3, iron is +3 (III). The same metal can have DIFFERENT charges in different compounds. That's exactly why you need the Roman numeral: to tell them apart.

#9: CoBr2
Hint

Co = cobalt (transition metal). Br = bromine (group 7A, charge = -1). There are 2 Br. Same method as CuCl2.

Full Answer
Br is -1. There are 2 Br: total negative = (-1)(2) = -2.
Co must be +2. Roman numeral = (II).
Br root + -ide = bromide.
Co = +2 | cobalt(II) bromide
#10: SnO2
Hint

Sn = tin (a metal that can have multiple charges, so treat it like a transition metal). O = oxygen (charge = -2). There are 2 O atoms. What must Sn's charge be?

Full Answer
O is -2. There are 2 O: total negative = (-2)(2) = -4.
Sn must be +4. Roman numeral = (IV).
O root + -ide = oxide.
Sn = +4 | tin(IV) oxide
Roman Numeral Cheat Sheet

I = +1, II = +2, III = +3, IV = +4. The Roman numeral IS the charge. Don't confuse it with the number of anions.

Section C: Write the Formula from the Name
The Criss-Cross Method

1. Write the cation charge and anion charge.

2. If the charges are equal (like +2 and -2), the ratio is 1:1. Done.

3. If the charges are NOT equal, criss-cross: the NUMBER from one charge becomes the SUBSCRIPT of the other ion.

4. Always check: multiply each charge by its subscript, add them up. Must equal zero.

5. Use parentheses around polyatomic ions ONLY when the subscript is greater than 1.

#11: Calcium chloride
Hint

Calcium = Ca (group 2A, charge = +2). "Chloride" = Cl (charge = -1). The charges are NOT equal (+2 vs -1). How many Cl do you need to balance +2?

Full Answer
Ca = +2. Cl = -1.
Charges not equal. Criss-cross: the 2 from Ca becomes Cl's subscript. The 1 from Cl becomes Ca's subscript (drop subscript of 1).
Check: (+2)(1) + (-1)(2) = +2 + (-2) = 0. Neutral.
Ca2+ | Cl- | CaCl2
#12: Iron(III) bromide
Hint

The (III) tells you iron's charge: +3. "Bromide" = Br, charge = -1. Charges not equal. How many Br to balance +3?

Full Answer
Fe = +3 (Roman numeral tells you). Br = -1.
Criss-cross: the 3 from Fe becomes Br's subscript. The 1 from Br becomes Fe's subscript (drop it).
Check: (+3)(1) + (-1)(3) = +3 + (-3) = 0. Neutral.
Fe3+ | Br- | FeBr3
#13: Aluminum oxide
Hint

Al = aluminum (group 3A, charge = +3). "Oxide" = O (charge = -2). Charges are BOTH greater than 1 and not equal. Criss-cross: the 3 goes to O's subscript, the 2 goes to Al's subscript.

Full Answer
Al = +3. O = -2.
Criss-cross: the 2 from O becomes Al's subscript (Al2). The 3 from Al becomes O's subscript (O3).
Check: (+3)(2) + (-2)(3) = +6 + (-6) = 0. Neutral.
Al3+ | O2- | Al2O3
This is the one you got wrong last time (AlCl)

On the last test, you wrote AlCl2 instead of AlCl3. Al is ALWAYS +3 (group 3A). When the anion is -1 (like Cl), you need 3 of them: AlCl3. When the anion is -2 (like O), you criss-cross: Al2O3.

#14: Magnesium nitrate
Hint

Mg = magnesium (group 2A, charge = +2). "Nitrate" = NO3 (look it up: charge = 1-). Charges not equal. How many NO3 to balance +2? Do you need parentheses?

Full Answer
Mg = +2. NO3 = -1.
Criss-cross: the 2 from Mg becomes NO3's subscript. Need 2 nitrate ions.
Parentheses? YES. There are 2 nitrate ions, so wrap in parentheses: (NO3)2.
Check: (+2)(1) + (-1)(2) = +2 + (-2) = 0. Neutral.
Mg2+ | NO3- | Mg(NO3)2
Parentheses Rule

When a polyatomic ion has a subscript greater than 1, you MUST put parentheses around it. Mg(NO3)2 means 2 copies of the entire NO3 group. Without parentheses, MgNO32 would mean something completely different.

#15: Calcium phosphate
Hint

Ca = +2. "Phosphate" = PO4 (charge = 3-). Different sizes: +2 and -3. Criss-cross. The 3 goes to Ca. The 2 goes to PO4. Parentheses around PO4?

Full Answer
Ca = +2. PO4 = -3.
Criss-cross: the 3 from PO4 becomes Ca's subscript (Ca3). The 2 from Ca becomes PO4's subscript ((PO4)2).
Parentheses? YES. More than one PO4, so wrap it.
Check: (+2)(3) + (-3)(2) = +6 + (-6) = 0. Neutral.
Ca2+ | PO43- | Ca3(PO4)2
This is like barium phosphate from last time

On the last test you wrote Ba(PO4) without criss-crossing. Ba is +2 and PO4 is -3, so you need 3 Ba and 2 PO4 to balance. Same pattern here with Ca. Always criss-cross when the charges are different sizes.
