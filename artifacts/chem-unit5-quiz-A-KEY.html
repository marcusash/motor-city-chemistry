Chemistry — Unit 5: Molecular Shape

Quiz [A] — Bonding Sites, Lone Pairs, Shape Names, and 3-D Drawing
Section 1 — Identify Bonding Sites and Lone Pairs (5.1.1)
1. BF3   (4 pts — 2 pts each)
a. Bonding sites on B: 3
b. Lone pairs on B: 0
Why:
Boron has only 3 valence electrons and forms 3 single bonds (one to each F). There are no electrons left over for a lone pair. Every electron boron has is used in a bond. This is one reason BF3 is unusual—B has an “incomplete octet.”
2. H2O   (6 pts — 2 pts each)
a. Bonding sites on O: 2
b. Lone pairs on O: 2
c. Total electron pairs around O: 4 (2 bonds + 2 lone pairs)
Why:
Oxygen has 6 valence electrons. It uses 2 to bond with the two H atoms (1 electron per bond shared with H). That leaves 4 electrons = 2 lone pairs. Total around O = 4 electron pairs, which gives it a tetrahedral domain geometry even though the shape is bent.
3. SO2   (6 pts — 2 pts each)
a. Bonding sites on S: 2  (double bond to one O = 1 site; single bond to other O = 1 site)
b. Lone pairs on S: 1
c. Difference from SO3: SO3 has 3 bonding sites and 0 lone pairs (trigonal planar, 120°); SO2 has 2 bonding sites and 1 lone pair (trigonal planar bent, <120°). The lone pair on S in SO2 changes both the shape name and the bond angle.
Section 2 — Name Molecular Shapes (5.1.2)
4. Fill in the blanks   (4 pts — 1 pt each blank)
a. 2 bonding sites, 0 lone pairs → Shape: Linear   Bond angle: 180°
b. 4 bonding sites, 0 lone pairs → Shape: Tetrahedral   Bond angle: 109.5°
Memory tip for Marcus:
Linear = 2 atoms pulled as far apart as possible = straight line. Tetrahedral = 4 bonds pushing equally in 3D space = 109.5° is the “perfect” spread of 4 equal forces in 3D. Think of CH4 as a pyramid with 3 base H’s and 1 top H; all angles equal.
5. 3 bonding sites, 1 lone pair   (6 pts — 2 pts each)
a. Shape name: Trigonal Pyramidal
b. Approximate bond angle: 107°
c. Why smaller than 120°: The lone pair creates a 4th electron domain around the central atom. With 4 total electron domains (3 bonds + 1 lone pair), the domain geometry is tetrahedral and the expected angle is 109.5°. But the lone pair repels more strongly than a bonding pair, compressing the bond angle from the tetrahedral ideal of 109.5° down to ~107°. It is smaller than 120° because the molecule has 4 electron domains, not 3.
Key concept:
Lone pairs are “fatter” than bonding pairs because they’re only attracted to one nucleus (the central atom) instead of two. They spread out more and squeeze the bonds. NH3 is the classic example: domain geometry is tetrahedral (4 pairs), so the base angle is 109.5°—not 120°. The lone pair then squeezes that down to ~107°. The shape name is trigonal pyramidal because we count only atoms, not lone pairs.

Molecule # Bonding Sites # Lone Pairs Shape Name

Pattern to notice:
CH4, NH3, and H2O all have 4 total electron pairs (domain geometry = tetrahedral). But as you replace bonding pairs with lone pairs, the shape changes and the bond angle gets smaller: 109.5° (CH4) → 107° (NH3) → 104.5° (H2O). This is a key pattern Kai should know cold.
Section 3 — Draw 3-D Structures (5.1.3)
7. CH4 — Tetrahedral 3-D Drawing   (8 pts)
Scoring rubric (8 pts):
1 pt — Wedge bond present and drawn as a filled/shaded triangle pointing toward viewer
1 pt — Dashed bond present and drawn as a dashed line pointing away from viewer
2 pts — Two regular lines drawn in the plane of the paper (left and right)
4 pts — H atom correctly placed at the end of each of the 4 bonds (1 pt each)
Accept any orientation as long as bond types are correctly labeled and central atom is C.
8. NH3 — Trigonal Pyramidal 3-D Drawing   (10 pts)
Sample explanation (full credit):
“NH3 is trigonal pyramidal, not tetrahedral, because one of the four electron pairs around N is a lone pair, not a bond. Molecular shape is named based on where the atoms are, not the lone pairs—so with only 3 atoms bonded to N, the shape is a triangular pyramid with N at the top, not a tetrahedron.”
Scoring rubric (10 pts):
3 pts — Drawing shows correct shape (N at top, 3 H at base, pyramidal)
3 pts — Bond types correctly labeled (at least 1 wedge distinguishable from regular bonds)
1 pt — Lone pair shown/labeled above N
3 pts — Explanation mentions: (1) lone pair present, (2) shape based on atom positions only, (3) correct shape name
